When several providers serve the same model, default routing ranks them by
expected cost per successful answer and shares traffic by that ranking: the
lowest cost wins the largest share. This page is for providers who want to
understand that ranking and win more of it.

## What the ranking measures

For each endpoint, Phaseo measures:

- **Effective price.** What a typical request for the model costs on your
  endpoint, using the model's real prompt and output sizes and the share of
  prompt tokens you actually serve from cache. A low input price does not help
  if output or cache reads cost more.
- **Time to first token**, counted from when Phaseo sends the request.
- **Output speed**, measured across the whole response, first token included.
- **Errors you cause**, such as `5xx` responses and streams that fail partway.
  These carry a heavy penalty.
- **Rate-limit rejections** (`429`). These carry a smaller penalty.

Waiting time is converted to dollars at the model's typical price per second,
so a faster endpoint can charge more and still rank first.

## How traffic is shared

- Providers within about 30% of the lowest cost per answer share most of the
  traffic. Being slightly behind costs only slightly.
- Beyond that band, share falls quickly.
- Every routable endpoint keeps a small share, so recoveries and improvements
  are noticed without any action from you.
- A conversation that is reading from your prompt cache usually stays on your
  endpoint while it remains healthy.

Requests that name a provider order, or ask to sort by price, latency or
throughput, do not use this ranking. See
[Routing and fallbacks](./routing-and-fallbacks.mdx).

## What earns more traffic

- Lower the price of a typical request, including output and cache reads.
- Serve repeated prompts from cache.
- Return the first token sooner and stream faster.
- Avoid errors and rate-limit rejections, and add capacity before demand
  reaches your limits.
